REVISION 2 — UNDERLYING ANIMATION REVIEW AND RESEARCH

Why the previous movement failed
Broad overlapping mesh fields moved faces, necks, hair and clothes together.
The mouth was being stretched instead of replaced by a drawn articulation.
Repeated periodic motion made held illustrations look rubbery. Those controls
were removed from the simple cut and were not reused in the hybrid.

Independent animation and visual-continuity critics reviewed source drawings,
native controls, camera crops and sequential generated frames. They identified
an excessively enlarged hand insert and a hesitant hover. Both were removed.
They also caught a word-end closure key overriding the next sung word. The
new per-frame exposure schedule fixes that conflict; chorus now has a separate
narrow mouth drawing. Superseded experimental frame caches were discarded.

Embrace
The raw 121-frame clip preserves her down-left gaze, passive hands and identity.
Contact forms around 2.3–2.5 s; the torso pull follows through about 3.6 s. The high
hand arc at 1.5–2.25 s is its weak point, with blurred fingers. The final edit cuts
to her unchanged expression over that interval, then returns for the pull.
The exact source endpoint begins the hold at global 39.0 s. The portrait camera
was revised to show the supporting hand rather than hide the key action.

Summer
The 121-frame clip contains changing sung/laughing mouth shapes, real closures
between phrases and a relaxed closed-mouth endpoint. Small generative changes
remain in his wristband and her clasped fingers/rings. These were judged minor
compared with the earlier facial/body warps. No further paid generation was
commissioned to chase those details.

Native singing and breathing
Four registered lip drawings are restricted to a small feathered mouth mask.
Exposure changes use CONSTANT interpolation, not a mouth dissolve or scaling.
Word-aligned vowel/consonant groups are approximate singing articulation.
A vocal stem and a manually refined phonetic exposure sheet would help with
closer synchronization if needed in a later production pass.
The breathing field is deliberately almost still. Some torso hair/jewellery
moves less than a pixel with the clothing; heads and background stay fixed.
The simple edit provides an entirely rigid fallback for comparison.
