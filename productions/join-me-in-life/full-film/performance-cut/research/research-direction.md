# Research for the movie-and-band recut

Retrieved 8 November 2026. This is production research for a fictional character, not an assessment of the user or a real individual. It supports the new directing brief; no clinic storyline, public-service narration or new ending is imposed by this record.

## The apparent calm is a supported concern, not a diagnostic reveal

[NIMH's Suicide FAQ](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/suicide-faq), revised2023, lists giving away valued belongings, arranging personal affairs, withdrawal and a sudden change from sadness to calm/happiness among warning signs. It also says prediction is difficult; risk factors do not mean a person will attempt suicide. Our artistic use should show a change in this particular woman's behavior alongside the already-established depression, emotional distance and burden/hopelessness expressed by the song. Simply cleaning cups or making a bed is not evidence that a person has decided to die. NIMH supports noticing concerning change and maintaining connection; it does not validate a universally recognizable “false peace” expression.

The [American Association of Suicidology warning-signs guide](https://suicidology.org/resources/warning-signs/) explicitly identifies sudden relief/calm after despair as potentially reflecting a decision rather than recovery, while emphasizing that no isolated sign establishes risk. New/intensifying changes or several signs together deserve attention. That permits her quiet certainty as authored character context, but cannot make her smile proof of an irreversible outcome. The same source rejects the idea that a determined person cannot be helped and describes connection as meaningful. The climax can be his dawning recognition of serious danger while the next moment remains open.

## Portrayal boundary that protects the intended drama

[Samaritans' drama/film guidance](https://www.samaritans.org/documents/1039/Guidance_on_depictions_of_suicide_and_self-harm_in_drama_and_film_Digital.pdf), ©2020,6pages, was read in full text through the current [drama guidance page](https://www.samaritans.org/about-samaritans/media-guidelines/guidance-portrayals-suicide-and-self-harm-drama/). It distinguishes complex, potentially preventable crisis from inevitable destiny. It discourages methods, romanticized suicidal acts, rewards through death and a portrayal of death as relief/peace. It also cautions against immediate uncomplicated recovery. Here the critical distinction is **apparent calm before danger versus peace supposedly achieved by dying**: show the former through fearful relational context, never validate the latter. Hope can be an unfinished interaction rather than a cure. For public release, source-of-support information can live in the accompanying description, without changing the original song or forcing a clinic scene.

[WHO's resource for filmmakers and stage/screen workers](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/preventing-suicide-a-resource-for-filmmakers-and-others-working-on-stage-and-screen), published9September2019, supports accurate, appropriate portrayals and notes that overcoming crisis/help-seeking narratives can offer protective messages. This supports preserving care and possible continued life. It does not require the film to resolve depression within four minutes. The accessible WHO overview was read; its linked full PDF returned403, so no detailed quotation or page-specific claim is attributed to the unread booklet.

The [Action Alliance messaging framework](https://suicidepreventionmessaging.org/safety/messaging-donts), read directly, reinforces avoiding a single-cause explanation, inevitability, romanticization and method imagery. Its guidance applies broadly to public communication; Samaritans is the more directly fiction-specific source here. No new warning banner or compulsory purchase is inferred from these sources.

## Practical screen translation — editorial inference, not a clinical prescription

1. **Show action and nonresponse in the same emotional unit.** She finishes an ordinary task with unusual deliberation; he appears or reaches into that space; the action ends but reciprocal attention does not begin. An uninterrupted beat after a completed task makes closure feel different from ordinary busyness.
2. **Give his fear an early point of view.** A change in his breathing, frozen approach, uncertain open hand or look from an ordered object to her face carries the alarm. Do not photograph her calm as bliss or make her an ominous villain. Avoid a radiant smile, halo-like backlight or beautiful imagined death.
3. **Use only a small cluster of objects.** Cups, bedding and the record/music object already belong to the lyrics. They can suggest settled finality without a checklist, explanatory letter, method object, timed countdown or explicit farewell instructions. Let gesture, silence between responses and his reaction carry the danger.
4. **Let the summer reveal what is missing.** Her wrong-word singing/laughter is alive, messy, reciprocal and spontaneous. Return from that one clearly bracketed memory to the same two people without her joining him. Repeating a comparable camera distance or shared glance is enough; additional golden hypothetical futures would create a third reality and dilute this contrast.
5. **Make the bridge recognition a transformation in his understanding.** The room and her stillness need not become more extreme. Reintroduce a relevant ordinary object/gesture from the opening, then her closed-off certainty, then his recognition. The first plea must already be forceful; subsequent pleas can expand into collective band force without turning the woman into a performer.
6. **Keep an opening for life at the end.** Continued company, an unfinished cup or a small self-chosen action can interrupt the earlier compulsion toward completion. Avoid a newly cheerful smile or a miraculous physical recovery. These are proposals to support the directors, not additional user-approved gestures.
