# Director B — An unfinished thing

Independent proposal, 8 November 2026. This is a fresh dramatic treatment and editorial challenge, not a new user decision or an accepted cut. ## The dramatic proposition

Make the audience afraid of how little she needs from the room. Her ordinary actions are calm and considerate, but she no longer answers his attempts at shared life. His fear is visible before the song names it. The summer memory later proves what reciprocal attention between these people used to look like. At the bridge, his earlier unease becomes recognition: she is treating their life as already finished. His pleas interrupt that certainty without acquiring power over her.

The useful governing image is an ordinary cup, not an invented visual universe. At the beginning, she finishes putting things in order. At the end, an ordinary cup is used and left imperfectly placed. That last action is hers. It is a small continuation, not proof she has recovered or agreed to live forever.

The renewed film has only three readable image categories:

- **Present:** the blue kitchen/apartment, real bodies and ordinary objects; she is withdrawn, he is frightened.
- **Memory:** the existing warm, exuberant private wrong-word singing/laughter passage. This is the only other time in their story.
- **Performance:** the five-member band in its fixed stage world; the song externalizes his effort. The band is not literally performing in her kitchen or watching her crisis on a screen.

Remove all golden possible futures, translucent windows, alternate breakfasts and seasons folding into pages. Warm practical light within an otherwise ordinary kitchen does not itself count as an alternate future.

## Opening: action, nonresponse, fear

A tidy table by itself is too ambiguous. An overlong smile can also read as actual recovery. Show the cluster in this particular woman: purposeful completion of ordinary tasks, residual depletion, absent reciprocal attention, and the reaction of someone who knows her.

Make **two new short moving shots**, rather than relabeling the old seated palm sweep as room organization:

1. **Records, about6 seconds:** a restrained three-quarter view of her finishing the alignment of a small ordinary record stack, then sliding it away from her active space. Her shoulders are low, expression nearly blank. No ominous letter, close-up of a destination, or instructional preparation. The object handling is one supported action with a visible finish; avoid a montage of complicated simultaneous chores.
2. **Cups, about6 seconds:** she sets one dry cup beside another and turns its handle into the same orientation. Her face remains visible enough to register fatigue. The movement ends cleanly, then her attention closes rather than opening toward him. A folded dishcloth may already be present; do not make a six-second generation wash, dry, stack and walk all at once.

These can occur at a counter/table without requiring a new full-body walking/sitting shot. A cut through performance permits a modest time ellipsis before the existing seated material, but the same room, garments, cups and screen sides must identify the continuity.

Then show his reaction **within the opening15–20 seconds**, not only after the first verse. C03's frightened offered hand/eyes or C06's searching face can supply it if their actual selected portions support the cut. Bring in the first band shot around the intro vocal hook, roughly13–16 seconds, after the unsettling action and a frightened human reaction have registered. The piano/heartbeat introduction can remain story-led; it does not need instant instrument coverage.

Do not add horror lighting, a tunnel vignette, an eerie supernatural smile or a literal black tunnel. Her attention can form the tunnel: she completes the object in front of her, looks through him, and returns to the task. He keeps trying to enter that attention and cannot.

## The climax needs a new acting standard

The renewed request makes the bridge stricter than the prior C acceptance. In the current C28 middle frame, her eyes are closed and she has a broad happy-looking smile. That was accepted contextually in the old film, but the new cut should not depend on the audience decoding four seconds of apparent pleasure as fatal certainty.

Preferred repair: one new restrained close in the established composition. She has the depleted face and downcast, contained attention of the present. She gives him a very brief practiced politeness, then looks past him. There is no luminous serenity. His reaction changes from searching to frightened recognition. Allow the ordinary breath and tiny gaze shift to play; do not ask a model for contradictory large emotional transitions in one short shot.

If regeneration is unnecessary after an actual cut test, C28's existing neutral opening or subdued ending may supply a short clean selection, with C26/C27 reactions carrying the rest. Never freeze or reverse the keeper. The first forceful plea then breaks this composure at the musical onset. Its strength comes from the cause we have understood, not a new strobe or symbolic effect.

The performance edit should resist an attractive temptation: cutting to all five band members on each of the four pleas. That spreads the visual attention away from the recognition the user explicitly names as the drama's peak. Put most of the band showcase in the choruses and synth region; protect the intimate bridge/first plea.

## Interpretation and review gates

Ordering possessions and an abrupt calm can be concerning **in a wider context**; the fiction must not teach that tidying proves a person has decided to die. This treatment uses the user's specified state for this particular woman, with withdrawal/burden context and a loved one's dread. The calm is not filmed as the reward, beauty or release of death. Continued connection and her own final small action/look leave a future available. Clinical details and primary-source wording should follow the research agent's consolidated record; relevant screen guidance is [Samaritans' drama and film guidance](https://www.samaritans.org/documents/1039/Guidance_on_depictions_of_suicide_and_self-harm_in_drama_and_film_Digital.pdf).

Before full generation, test the opening and bridge as coherent musical sequences with provisional sources. Pass only if an independent viewer can describe: she is putting things in order but not reaching toward him; he is already scared; the summer is one real remembered past; the bridge makes his recognition sharper; the pleas are caring desperation rather than triumph or control; the last action and tearful look are hers and do not claim a cure. Also ask where the band belongs emotionally, not merely whether all instruments appeared.
