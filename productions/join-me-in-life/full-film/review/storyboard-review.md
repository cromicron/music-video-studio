# Independent storyboard and free-motion review

6 November 2026. Reviewer: audio/story agent, separate from the storyboard author and editor. Scope: the nine-panel board, all nine extracted PNGs at native size, A01/A02 references, the current full-film edit schedule, the supplied LTX contact sheet, and an additional sequential extraction from the LTX opening.

**Disposition: useful full-song planning artwork, with corrections and clear limits. It is not final detailed art or evidence of finished animation.** No normal-speed playback with sound was performed. This report does not grant user creative approval.

## Highest-priority findings

1. **Every new panel is only 546–551 × 306 pixels.** The whole board is 1672 × 941. A full-frame 1920 × 1080 display magnifies the panels by about 3.5 times in each dimension before any camera push. Thin hair, eyelids, freckles, fingers and distant props cannot support the same final detail as A01/A02. A 720p animatic is a reasonable planning use; upscale output dimensions do not restore source detail. Final shot art should be expanded/refined from the selected board composition, inspected again, and then animated.
2. **`night_room`, right half: the bed is plainly rumpled.** It does not depict the carefully made bed in Verse 1. S008's cup insert can keep the intended emphasis off the bed, but the wide image must not be described as an exact illustration of that lyric. The final morning room can intentionally be untidy; the current night/morning pair lacks that contrast.
3. **`night_room` → `morning_room` is a thematic pair, not a continuity match.** The nearest cup's handle flips from the left to the right; mug shapes/spacing, record sleeves, the bed and framing differ. Do not dissolve/morph between them as though the camera and objects were fixed overnight. A separated hard cut is safer for the animatic.
4. **The final edit moves outside, back inside, then outside again.** S051 (`dawn_together`) → S052 (`dawn_waiting`) → S053 (`dawn_together`) can read as spatial/time discontinuity. Keep the later morning beats in a clear progression, or explicitly treat these as an associative montage. The outside bench is a proposed later scene, not an established continuation of the original hospital cut.
5. **No unambiguous gross extra-limb defect is visible in the nine stills, but hand anatomy is not certified.** `his_arrival`'s grip and `dawn_together`'s overlapping hands are too small/occluded for a final finger-level pass. Their apparent plausibility at 550-pixel width is insufficient for a detailed final master.

## Comparison with the established direction

The storyboard broadly preserves red/copper hair, green eyes, freckles, black T-shirt over purple striped sleeves, blue jeans, silver pendant, his dark alternative clothing, tousled brown hair and light facial hair. It keeps her withdrawn and him concerned. The new dawn image avoids an obvious cure/rescue pose. These are strengths of the planning board, not proof of universal identity consistency in future motion.

The first scene's hospital exterior, interrupted bedroom, journey, waiting room, warm summer memory, four pleas and dawn make a comprehensible overall arc. The current schedule has 58 shot segments from 18 distinct still sources. `her_window` appears eight times for about 37.6 seconds. A cut to a different crop does not create a new performance, and the repeated stills will remain visibly an animatic even with smooth camera moves. This is an honest and useful planning deliverable if labelled that way.

The four screams are assigned four separate beats. S044 and S046 use the same plea drawing at different scales; S045 and S047 use her stillness/the shared hospital space. That can work as a storyboard structure. It does not yet demonstrate escalation in actual acting or precise onset sync. The ASR-derived starts remain estimates requiring listening.

## Independent LTX pilot inspection

Source: `research/free-test/LTX25_waitingroom_free_5s.mp4`, observed 896 × 512, 24 fps, 121 video frames, video duration 5.041667 s. It also contains an audio stream; a future usable picture clip should use the real song, not automatically inherit generated source audio.

Viewed the supplied whole-clip contact sheet and created [opening sequence](ltx-opening-sequence.jpg) with samples at 0.00, 0.08, 0.16, 0.24, 0.32, 0.40, 0.48, 0.56, 0.64, 0.80, 1.00 and 1.50 s.

**I corroborate rejection for this story.** Specific observations:

- During roughly the first 0.16–0.40 s, his head rotates from looking toward/down beside her to facing the viewer. The in-between face/hair becomes soft and the head/neck outline changes noticeably while the torso is comparatively fixed. This is not a clean, restrained held performance.
- By roughly 0.4–0.5 s he is looking directly outward, and the later contact-sheet samples sustain that pose. The action pulls attention away from her and changes the intended relationship of the shot.
- The face settles into a simpler, narrower, more generic frontal drawing. Facial-hair and hair-line detail no longer have the same fidelity as the opening/reference. Her eye/gaze also changes early; the frame sequence does not establish subtle natural acting.
- The frames do not show a clear hand catastrophe, because hands/action are largely outside the important visible region. Absence of that one defect would not outweigh the unwanted turn and identity drift.

This finding is based on sequential frame evidence, **not normal-speed audiovisual playback**. I am not claiming that every intermediate frame was reviewed or that the clip contains a mechanically detached head. The visible pose/identity change is enough to reject its use in this cut. Technical full decode alone cannot reverse that finding.

## Completion boundary

The 277.776-second animatic can be reviewed for story order, coverage and proposed pacing. It cannot pass the requested finished-animation standard until final-resolution artwork, approved actual actions, normal-speed listening/viewing, and shot-level motion review exist. The rejected pilot should remain evidence, not silently become filler in the render.
