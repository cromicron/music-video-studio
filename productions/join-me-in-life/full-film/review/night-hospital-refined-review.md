## What holds up

- **Composition and mood:** the large snow-covered tree and empty bench remain left, the warm entrance remains right of center, the low canopy and cleared reflective path remain the destination, and the isolated lit upper-right window remains. The cold blue exterior/warm amber interior still reads as the same winter night. No new person, ambulance, emergency spectacle, or story resolution is introduced.
- **Drawn treatment:** deliberate dark outlines, painted snow, graphic blue wall panels and stylized foliage remain compatible with the detailed illustrated world. The greater surface detail does not turn this into a photographic location plate.
- **Architecture:** canopy edges, façade seams and entrance framing are broadly coherent at the shown view; no obvious floating roof or impossible major building joint. The wet pavement's amber bands plausibly continue the entrance light. Glass and interior lighting remain readable instead of producing a false reflected person.
- **Furniture:** the bench back/seat and visible supporting metal frame make a plausible single object. Its partial occlusion by snow/vegetation is reasonable. Interior chairs now resolve into recognizable seats with backrests and legs rather than the original blurred marks; no conspicuous merged chair or unsupported seat was visible at this scale.

## Changes and limits

This is a **redraw, not a pixel-exact upscale**. Branch forks, snow clumps/flakes, bench detailing, canopy lights, wall seams, curtain/window detail, interior chairs, plant detail, glazing/handles and pavement reflections have been reconstructed. The original is too small to establish an exact matching chair count or door mechanism. These newly definite details must not be described as recovered original pixels. Macro placement is close, not identical; the aspect ratio also changes slightly (about 1.791 to 1.777), so use aspect-preserving fit/crop rather than stretching.

The central/lateral glass panels contain several overlapping vertical framing and handle planes. They can plausibly represent a glazed entrance/vestibule, but the still does **not** unambiguously establish which leaves move, hinge or slide, or how the successive glass planes separate. This is acceptable for an exterior view, not a reliable mechanical reference for opening doors. Do not animate handles or door leaves without first resolving that topology. Keep chair, bench, canopy and wall geometry stable in any generated move; check light/reflection shimmer and snow motion throughout the resulting clip.

A snow-covered approach becoming cleared/wet immediately at the entrance is not inherently inconsistent.
