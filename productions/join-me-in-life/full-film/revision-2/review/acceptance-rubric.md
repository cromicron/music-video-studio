# Revision 2 — independent story and animation acceptance

Prepared 7 November 2026 by the outside story critic. **Option A's revised causal spine has passed only for rough storyboarding; see the [scoped treatment gate](treatment-a-review.md). No animatic or finished animation has passed.** Dario's new brief is an entirely new storyboard, a sophisticated story matched to the whole original song, and convincing animation without repetition used as padding.

## What the first film teaches

I read the [original lyrics](../../../full-song/lyrics.txt), the [timing map and its confidence limits](../../analysis/timing.json), and all 58 shot descriptions in the [v1 edit](../../edit-production-candidate.json). I visually inspected 60 selected first/middle/last frames covering 20 shots in chronology sheets [1](../../review/final-full-film-review/chronology-01.jpg), [5](../../review/final-full-film-review/chronology-05.jpg), [8](../../review/final-full-film-review/chronology-08.jpg), [11](../../review/final-full-film-review/chronology-11.jpg) and [14](../../review/final-full-film-review/chronology-14.jpg). This is a sampled visual/story audit, not fresh full-speed viewing, listening, exhaustive motion review or a new technical rejection of the master.

The preserved identities, cold/warm contrast, frightened male performance, withdrawn female reaction and maintained embrace are strengths. The old technical/continuity PASS establishes a playable, mostly coherent assembly under its declared scope. It does not establish that the film develops enough dramatically to satisfy the new brief.

- **The opening spends its curiosity too slowly.** S001–S006 occupy 33 seconds with establishment, empty-room returns, portrait and corridor. The viewer has little visible cause for his subsequent journey; the lyrics carry the missing story.
- **The objects are largely illustrations.** The room/records/cups recur in S002/S004/S007/S008, then at the bridge and dawn. Their light and framing change, but we do not witness a choice that changes what those objects mean. A new pan or floating dust would retain this weakness.
- **Contact becomes an editorial stopping point.** The held pose is important, but repeated returns through the second chorus and bridge principally sustain the same state. The film needs a new obstacle, attempt, revelation or decision around that contact, without adding a second embrace or changing her established withdrawal.
- **The instrumental changes scenery more than situation.** S036–S039 move through empty stage, summer, hospital and corridor. The passage can do narrative work: connect a memory to a present action, reveal what he has missed, or prepare a specific later choice.
- **Dawn arrives more clearly than an ending is earned.** The last minute brings warmer rooms and company outside. A small visible action that could not have occurred at the start would make continuation meaningful without depicting a cure.

## Gates: evidence before a pass

| Gate | Question and required evidence | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Causal story — treatment** | Can the reviewer explain who wants what, what prevents it, what each tries, and how that changes the next action? Mark the initiating event, at least one consequential turn, the crisis and an earned final choice on the song timeline. | Scenes can be shuffled without altering the story; the same description explains several consecutive sections. |
| **2. Legible action — board** | Each sequence has a visible before-state, playable action and after-state. Show enough poses/framing to understand the action without its caption. A change of attention, hesitation or refusal counts when visible and consequential. | The treatment says someone decides or understands, but the picture only shows an unchanged face; animation is merely blinking, hair or camera movement. |
| **3. Musical development — animatic** | Each chorus has a different dramatic situation or consequence. The four screams are urgent from the first, with a designed progression in response, scale, interruption or consequence. Preserve phrase anticipation and reaction time; verify important cue onsets against the recording. | A chorus restarts the same action, bigger gestures are the only escalation, or all cuts follow a mechanical fixed duration/beat pattern. The planning timing map alone is treated as exact listening evidence. |
| **4. Motif payoff — board/animatic** | For every recurring object/location, name what the viewer learns or feels differently at its return. At least the ending's central motif must gain meaning from an observed action. | Repetition is defended only as symbolism, a lyric illustration or a match in colour; the return communicates nothing new. |
| **5. Scene geography and agency — board/animatic** | A fresh reviewer can distinguish present, memory and performance; track who sees what, where the characters are and why the next scene follows. Both characters have specific behaviour; his support does not erase her agency or turn him into an assured rescuer. | Location/time changes need the treatment to explain them, eye-lines reverse relationships, or recovery/death is inferred against the agreed premise. |
| **6. Convincing motion — source and actual edit** | Review every retained action at normal speed and inspect its difficult phases: initiation, weight transfer, contact, release and settle. Track hands, limbs, facial identity, props, gaze and reflections, then review the incoming/outgoing cuts. | Foot skating, anatomy changes, unexplained grip resets, prop teleporting, accidental laughter/reassurance, character duplication or emotional-role reversal survives in the used interval. Minor detail softness is assessed at viewing scale, not by demanding impossible still-frame perfection. |
| **7. Earned duration — animatic/final** | For every pause, ask what anticipation, tension, observation or consequence the viewer experiences during it. Audit loops, reverse playback, repeated source intervals, retiming and frozen extensions separately. | A clip ends before its slot and is repeated/frozen to fill the music; successive shots add no meaningful information. A unique generated frame, percentage animated, or low duplicate count is presented as proof of good storytelling. |
| **8. Whole-song finish — final** | Play the exact full cut with the untouched original song, review actual cuts and sound/picture timing, and identify where tension rises, releases and changes. End on visible continuation rather than a cure. Verify the actual shared delivery file. | Only isolated clips pass; a scene's strength depends on the reviewer knowing the production explanation; the climax or ending remains a sequence of attractive waiting poses. |

Designed stillness, held drawings and quiet performances are valid animation choices. They pass through their dramatic purpose, not an exemption invented after footage runs out. Conversely, continuous motion is not a story. The target is deliberate screen time throughout the song.

## Independent procedure

1. **Treatment:** another reviewer challenges the causal chain before final art. Record exact weak beats and revise; do not commission all shots merely because the summary sounds emotional.
2. **Animatic:** show the board cut at song length to a fresh reviewer without the synopsis, shot-purpose captions or the maker's desired interpretation. First ask open questions: “What happened?”, “What changed between them?”, “Where were you uncertain?”, “Which passage felt repetitive?” Then compare the answers to the intended turning points. Record what the reviewer actually viewed/heard; screenshots are not full audiovisual viewing.
3. **Pilot actions:** test the story's most consequential interaction and its adjacent cuts, not only easy atmosphere. A failed motion test triggers revised staging or a changed take; it does not quietly replace the beat with a held portrait.
4. **Final:** all eight gates need an evidenced pass, with no unresolved story or movement blocker. Record minor limitations separately. Technical decode/hash checks remain necessary but cannot overrule a story failure. No numerical average can hide a failed gate, and an agent's pass never substitutes for Dario's creative judgment.

## Craft sources used

- [Walt Disney Animation — Story](https://disneyanimation.com/process/story/): storyboards develop emotion, expression, timing, staging and framing together, moving from rough thumbnails to clearer drawings. Application here: judge visible sequences before polishing isolated images.
- [Walt Disney Animation — Animation](https://www.disneyanimation.com/process/animation/): believable character performance depends on staging, timing, anticipation, follow-through, anatomy and weight; both subtle and broad movement serve emotion. Application here: assess the purpose and mechanics of an action rather than its motion quantity.
- [Walter Murch in conversation with Steve Hullfish](https://www.provideocoalition.com/aotc-murch-books/amp/): Murch prioritizes emotion, story and rhythm over literal continuity, describes previewing as a way to expose editorial blind spots, and distinguishes decisive turning points from ordinary scene changes. Application here: retain physical credibility, but do not confuse flawless joins with a compelling film; use an unseeded story read and identify what becomes different after each turn.

These sources inform the review method. They do not provide evidence that this film or any planned revision has passed it.
