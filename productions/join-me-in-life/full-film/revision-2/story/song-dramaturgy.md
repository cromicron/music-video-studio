## What the old cut proved, and what it did not

The 58-shot film uses 20 source keys. Twenty-four shots are still artwork with camera movement, totaling 115.291667 seconds. First/end holds add 9.708333 seconds: **125 seconds, or 45.0% of the film**, use a still or explicit hold. Six empty-environment sources occupy **98.875 seconds / 35.59%**. These categories overlap; they must not be added together.

The motion-source interval comparison finds **zero overlapping reused source frames**. Calling it a film of looped LTX takes would be inaccurate. The dominant repetition is the return to the same drawings, composition and dramatic state. Nine after-contact shots alone occupy 37.208333 seconds, and even distinct, plausible head movements frequently communicate the same fact. Generating another 125 seconds of blinks in the same staging would miss Dario's criticism.

The summer exchange, coherent embrace, winter/summer contrast, recognisable characters and the full original song are useful foundations. The first scene nevertheless lacks an event that starts the story; the first chorus produces no clear choice; the synth solo changes scenery without changing the situation; the screams escalate performance without a new consequence; the ending mainly changes light. The earlier technical and scoped motion passes remain true. They do not establish that this dramatic structure is sufficiently compelling.

## The new causal spine

**A frightened man reaches a woman who is quietly putting her life in order. He first tries to find the words that will make everything right; through the night he learns to make room for her next choice. She does not recover on cue. She chooses one threshold, then another, and finally enters professional care while he keeps his promise to stay.**

The visible question is no longer only “will she still be sad in the next shot?” It becomes “will they get through this particular door, and what must he do differently to help without taking the choice from her?” The domestic door, hospital entrance and consultation doorway each mean something different. No threatening height, explicit self-harm method, death reveal or invented miraculous cure is needed.

Make the first journey **to her home**, then let the pair travel to the hospital. This is a proposed full-film change, not a claim that the preserved Short already shows it. Keep the established hospital relationship and the one continuous embrace. The Short's exact captions, frames and cuts remain untouched as an archived separate work; the new full-film sequence earns its own continuity review.

The first chorus means **come with me**. The second means **I will remain here with you**. The final chorus means **I can stay while you choose help**. Repeated words acquire different visible consequences. The new ending is a first practical step and shared endurance, not a medical outcome.

## Four motifs that change meaning

| Motif | First meaning | Changed meaning | Payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Door / threshold | A private world closing; an offer that can be refused. | A shared entrance, then a feared consultation step. | She chooses entry; later he waits for an invitation instead of forcing access. |
| Records / music | She gives away something personal. | Summer reveals why their imperfect shared music matters. | Sister keeps the record box nearby; nothing has to be put definitively away tonight. |
| Two cups | A domestic task made unnaturally complete. | A practical offer that need not be accepted immediately. | Hospital cups remain unfinished because life and attention are still in progress. They are analogous props, not the same mugs teleported between locations. |
| Hand / space | A hand offered before she is ready. | One genuine embrace, then practical support with room to withdraw. | Her own planted feet and decision carry the threshold; his hand is available rather than controlling. |

## Staging and musical grammar

Use approximately 42–48 shots, often 4–7 seconds, with a small number of longer continuous actions. This is a planning target, not a quota to fill. A shot must contain a readable event, response or motivated continuation. Atmospheric wide shots can breathe while snow, walking or another meaningful process unfolds; they should not replace missing events.

Choose a consistent home-to-hospital screen direction. Set the consultation door and chair positions in one clear wide view before the solo. Reverse angles require an eyeline or neutral establishing shot. Keep the record sleeve, box contents, cup colours, coat state and which hand holds which object in a continuity ledger. Leave headroom and full support anatomy for stand/sit/walk shots; do not ask a face close-up to prove foot placement.

Concentrate musical performance imagery into a small formal lane. Introduce the rehearsal/performance space in the opening so the bridge does not suddenly invent another literal location. Present-day narrative need not mimic every syllable. If a visible mouth is singing, condition and inspect it against the exact source excerpt; otherwise use nonverbal narrative acting. “Audio included” is not evidence of lip synchronisation.

Cut on completed decisions and musical phrasing, not mechanically on every bar. A visual reaction can precede a lyric so the audience makes the connection. Use the summer match cut once with a changed emotional meaning; do not keep revisiting the same five-second exchange. The solo earns abstraction or visual scale only when the audience still understands what is at stake.

## What must be proven before the full batch

The hardest new actions are two-person record handoff, putting on a coat, walking together at different pace, rising then sitting back, voluntary stand, and door entry with another person. Trial those as bounded clips using clear starting/ending states. Keep the ambitious story while changing coverage if a complex shot fails: for example, separate the stable box handoff from a reaction, or cover the coat already taken with its subsequent shoulder adjustment. Do not replace the act with a cut to its unexplained result or add a freeze to fill the song.

An independent viewer of the storyboard/animatic, without this treatment, should be able to answer: what triggered his arrival; what she chose in the first chorus; why summer matters; what interrupted entry during the solo; what changes on the final chorus; and why he is still there at dawn. Missing answers require story changes before another GPU batch.

The finished cut must additionally show no reset embrace, no unmotivated object teleport, no repeated source interval, no frozen endpoint padding, and credible feet/hands during the new actions. Review actual neighbouring cuts at normal speed with the original song. Technical frame count, smooth blinks and a successful encode remain necessary checks, not the creative pass criterion.
