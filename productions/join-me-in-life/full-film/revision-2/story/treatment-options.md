# Two new full-song story treatments

Proposals by the story-research agent, 7 November 2026. These are new narrative constructions, not a relabeling of the first film. They are not independently approved, not generated footage, and not new user-confirmed canon. The song director independently converged on the grounded spine of Option A; the two agents exchanged concrete staging ideas after their initial work.

Both preserve the adult protagonists, his fear for her, her withdrawal, the winter hospital premise, the reciprocal summer memory, the one present-day embrace with her looking away, and all four screamed pleas. No injury, attempt, death, cure or competent romantic savior is invented. The four pleas are vocal performance/interior expression rather than four literal confrontations shouting at a distressed woman. The original master is not rearranged. Section times below are the existing planning map; most boundaries are not phoneme-accurate and must be checked against the actual recording before final cut placement.

## Recommend A: One More Door

**Logline:** A man races to reach the woman he loves, then discovers that staying beside her matters more than finding the perfect thing to say. Across one winter night, she makes a series of small, difficult choices that finally take her through the door to care; he stays on the other side.

**Dramatic engine:** His objective evolves from *get there* to *persuade* to *accompany* to *make room for help*. Her action evolves from closing down her ordinary world to allowing company, tolerating the wait, and making her own step. The external obstacle is the distance and then a frightening threshold; the internal obstacle is her withdrawal and his fear-driven wish to control the ending.

The story begins at home, not with 30 seconds of empty establishing images. The sister is a brief supporting character with one causally important act: she recognizes the unusual gift and calls him. No readable phone messages, diagnostic labels or written exposition are required. She need not recur or become a separate subplot.

### Why A is the production recommendation

It gives the existing song three increasingly meaningful thresholds and makes both characters necessary. Most new actions can be staged in readable 4–7-second units: call/answer, take coat, wait/open door, match pace, rise/sit, approach/stop, step through, return to chair. The story remains clear even if a complex continuous two-person interaction needs several shots. The camera can grow from rigid domestic order, through urgent pursuit, toward steadier shared space. It does not need random spectacle to become sophisticated.

Specific risks to test: the sister's introduction can confuse identity; keep her design/wardrobe distinct and her function brief. The repeated threshold idea must not become repeated footage. The cups payoff can become too literal or falsely hopeful; preserve ordinary uncertainty and show care continuing. The bridge associations need tight timing so the story does not appear to rewind. A long chair/door outro still fails unless the stated actions actually occur.

## Alternative B: The Unfinished Page

**Logline:** While waiting at the hospital, a frightened musician keeps seeing their lives as a finished illustrated tragedy. Memories and the woman's small present actions contradict his imagined script, until he stops trying to write the ending and leaves her a blank next page.

This is a formally different, nonlinear music-film proposal. The present-day waiting room is the frame story; home, journey and summer are revealed through illustrated spaces that open from ordinary objects. Page borders are his visual metaphor, never a diagnosis or an objective supernatural event. No corpse, harmful method or acted fatal ending is shown. This approach requires more art-direction and transition testing than A and is **not** the cheaper or safer default merely because it can hide imperfect action behind effects.

### Why B is the alternative, not the selected plan

It gives the lyric's story/theatre language a coherent formal system and can produce striking, rewatchable transitions. But the book is a proposed new prop, not established canon, and the formal system can overwhelm a sensitive human story. Page-space morphs, duplicated characters, symbolic panels and active graphic transitions add generation risks; generic drifting pages would be decorative padding. It must pass a blind readability test explaining present vs memory vs subjective fear before full production. If viewers infer death, magical rescue, or that he controls her choice, the staging fails.

## Selection and checks

I recommend **A** with perhaps one or two carefully motivated graphic matches from B, not the entire book apparatus. Following the outside critic's causal challenge, A now gives her a specific refusal at the second chorus and reserves the first approach to care for the solo; this proposal still needs that critic's actual readback. Preserve the emotional sophistication in behavior and editing. Start by boarding the entire song, especially the 32-second intro and 30-second outro, then make the three interaction tests named in [the research method](../research/music-video-story-research.md). Do not authorize the treatment through a tally of generated frames: the independent story reader must be able to describe the changes at the choruses and the final choice without the written treatment.
