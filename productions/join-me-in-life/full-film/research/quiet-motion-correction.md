## What the sources establish

- The official [LTX-2.3 prompt guide](https://ltx.io/blog/ltx-2-3-prompt-guide), **10 March 2026**, tells I2V users to describe the desired motion rather than redundantly restating the image. It recommends observable physical cues, chronological action and enough direction for the requested duration. Its claims about longer prompts concern useful detail, not padding with prohibitions. Short quote: “Express emotion through physical cues, not abstract labels.”
- The official [adherence guide](https://ltx.io/blog/how-to-improve-ltx-2-3-prompt-adherence), **13 May 2026**, favours a clear primary action, literal movement and controlled complexity. It explicitly permits bypassing prompt enhancement for exact creative control. Its broad CFG/STG advice is not a reason to change our distilled checkpoint’s prescribed settings.
- The official [common-mistakes guide](https://ltx.io/blog/common-prompt-mistakes-in-ai-video-generation), **13 May 2026**, warns about unspecified motion, competing descriptions and too many elements; it suggests separate clips for complex scenes. It does **not** demonstrate that splitting a quiet six-second pose prevents unwanted gestures.
- The [Lightricks 2.3 model card](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.3) specifies distilled variants at **eight steps, CFG 1**. The current [official distilled pipeline](https://github.com/Lightricks/LTX-2/blob/4f521619ae0029e8fbb64d555030c2b121a33b4f/packages/ltx-pipelines/src/ltx_pipelines/distilled.py#L284), pinned to commit `4f521619ae0029e8fbb64d555030c2b121a33b4f` (**4 November 2026**), encodes `[prompt]` only and exposes no separate negative-prompt argument. File SHA-256: `481164fb15f57cc56c4f51f34d7759a0f4ffe7ca656a7e6ddc50c44bb7cf25c2`. This verifies the official path; the GPU agent is separately checking our pinned WanGP adaptation.
- The current [general prompt guide](https://docs.ltx.io/open-source-model/usage-guides/prompting-guide) recommends a short flowing single-shot paragraph with concrete actions. Its new duration-predictor/multi-shot sections are explicitly **2.5**, not established 2.3 capabilities. Similarly, the current [I2V tutorial](https://docs.ltx.io/open-source-model/usage-guides/image-to-video) now describes **2.5/ComfyUI**; do not import its enhancer/default negative field into this frozen 2.3 runtime. These living pages were read on the check date and show no publication date.

## Negation: mechanism versus hypothesis

“No reaching, touch, embrace, hand lift…” inside the **positive** paragraph remains positive-channel conditioning; it is not a separate exclusion control. A long list also spends conditioning detail naming several competing actions instead of specifying the small permitted action. At ordinary CFG 1, the usual conditional/unconditional combination reduces to the conditional prediction; adding a negative list to an unused branch cannot repair this. Do not raise CFG or introduce NAG as part of this wording test.

**Hypothesis, not an official proven rule:** naming many forbidden social gestures may make those concepts more salient and give the model opportunities to complete a familiar dramatic scene. I found no official 2.3 negation-ablation experiment proving that those words caused our failure, and it would be wrong to claim LTX always ignores “no.” The concrete evidence is narrower: [our rejected L04](../review/ltx23-quiet-L04-review.md) raises its forearm from f80, touches its mouth, extends a palm, then returns despite matching endpoint guides. Replacing that vocabulary with persistent support/contact positions is a testable correction, not guaranteed control.

These describe already established support, rather than asking the model to perform a new hug or put hands into a new position. Room tone is merely positive conditioning for the joint model; generated audio remains discarded and the original song remains the edit’s soundtrack. Neither text is tested yet.
