## Preserve the source performance

[Continuity review](../review/short-to-full-continuity.md) identifies M01's defect at **1.5–2.25 s**, worst approximately **1.7–2.1 s**; contact around 2.3–2.5 s also needs inspection. M01 is 1916×1080, 24 fps, 121 frames / 5.041667 s, SHA-256 `bb6e21e887c5b86d815c1b2638896f2b2612471238d3dd2ee798b586418d5246`. R4 uses five seconds. Its lead-in, pull and held aftermath provide the temporal context that the failed whole-action regeneration did not retain reliably.

Use the complete 121-frame source as temporal conditioning; it already satisfies 8n+1. A matching lossless per-frame mask should be white only over the defect and black over retained footage. Cover the swept hand, wrist and necessary sleeve/occlusion context, with deliberate boundary room. A finger-only mask can repair finger appearance; it cannot also lower the high arm arc when the arm remains outside the mask. Keep her face, passive hands, away gaze and both neck/shoulder connections outside the repair unless a reviewed overlap is unavoidable. No cutaway, loop, reverse, second reach, camera reset or shortened action.

After generation, composite only the approved region and its finite feather boundary into the **original native frames**, with black-mask frames restored wholly from the source. Verify equality outside that declared region before lossy delivery encoding, then review the entire reach→contact→pull→hold at normal speed. Lower-resolution inference may make a cheaper diagnostic, but it does not certify native hand detail; never upscale/reconstruct the whole retained picture unnecessarily. Keep the original soundtrack/master and timing. This remains **ChatGPT → owned LTX runner → FFmpeg**; ComfyUI supplies reference code, not a fourth creative application in the proposed production route.

## What the owned pinned backend already has

WanGP commit `b8880bd5d292edfbe2384f5c951d95757ad59ea0` is preserved by the [A2V source lock](../tools/kaggle-a2v/source-lock.json).

- [`ltx2.py:1023`](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/WanGP/blob/b8880bd5d292edfbe2384f5c951d95757ad59ea0/models/ltx2/ltx2.py#L1023) builds temporal control entries from `input_frames` and a `masking_source` from `input_masks`. The incoming `masking_source` argument is overwritten; callers must use the actual inputs. `video_prompt_type` must contain `G` or masking strength is reset to zero.
- [`helpers.py:397`](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/WanGP/blob/b8880bd5d292edfbe2384f5c951d95757ad59ea0/models/ltx2/ltx_pipelines/utils/helpers.py#L397), `prepare_mask_injection` / `_apply_mask_injection`, encode the original sequence, map masks onto the causal latent lattice and reinsert noisy source at black-mask tokens for `ceil(steps × strength)` steps; white tokens remain generated. This is **real temporal source preservation in latent space**, not just a prompt. It thresholds at 0.5, nearest-resizes temporally, and pads missing mask frames white: exact full-length masks and pixel-space restoration are essential.
- [`distilled.py`](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/WanGP/blob/b8880bd5d292edfbe2384f5c951d95757ad59ea0/models/ltx2/ltx_pipelines/distilled.py) invokes mask injection in both stages. However, [`ltx2.py:1231`](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/WanGP/blob/b8880bd5d292edfbe2384f5c951d95757ad59ea0/models/ltx2/ltx2.py#L1231) retains the control-video guide in stage 2 only when `phase2_ic_lora_name(...)` finds an active IC-LoRA multiplier. This requires deliberate wiring.
- [`ltx2_handler.py:494`](https://github.com/deepbeepmeep/WanGP/blob/b8880bd5d292edfbe2384f5c951d95757ad59ea0/models/ltx2/ltx2_handler.py#L494) explicitly returns **“LTX2 outpainting doesnt support Video Mask.”** It also forces outpainting to one phase. Its configured adapter is the older `ltx-2.3-22b-ic-lora-outpaint.safetensors`, not the official newer in/outpainting file. No equivalent official green-mask/two-stage pyramid-blend path was found in this pinned implementation. Bypassing its UI guard would not establish compatibility.

The newer adapter is already named in the public metadata of our pinned converted repository, `DeepBeepMeep/LTX-2@1798f80b3340d2f87cffae42f9fa5367d0963d0c`: **1,308,778,338 bytes**, SHA-256 `73dd0841c0d4f0eb26fb1f017781b841b2752021944ac5ecefe57917f6dae6b5`. This exactly matches [official Hub metadata](https://huggingface.co/api/models/Lightricks/LTX-2.3-22b-IC-LoRA-In-Outpainting?blobs=true), revision `8becd61405133263d9e580be41a92e8f827b01ed`. The older outpaint file has a different hash, `32c5d3e0649aa4e89b192319f3c79460dfd2319d2859ca11fa6f88e983a81665`. Metadata establishes file identity, not working inference. Official access is gated and the adapter uses the LTX-2 community license; a public converted copy does not remove the original terms. Neither adapter is downloaded or included in the diagnostic allowlist.

## Precision, memory and cost

**Cheapest credible development route:** reuse the owned dual-T4 Q4 runtime if its actual A2V hardware test passes, then port only the 2.3 preprocessing, selected adapter, source conditioning and blend sequence into a separate repair runner. Existing precision decisions are FP16/Q4 transformer, BF16/int8 Gemma and FP32 video encoder/decoder/upsampler. T4 lacks native BF16 tensor-core support; blanket FP16 conversion previously risks Gemma overflow. Q4 plus this LoRA, temporal guide tokens, two-stage intermediate decode/re-encode and native M01 resolution have **no measured T4 peak-memory/runtime evidence**. Two 16 GB T4s are not one pooled 32 GB device. Successful image/audio conditioning alone would not establish repair capacity or quality.

**Established alternative:** run the official 2.3 graph on a larger GPU. The [pinned node README](https://github.com/Lightricks/ComfyUI-LTXVideo/blob/220e1d45aa0469f518e982102fca4dca92a61862/README.md#low-vram) claims **32 GB VRAM** with sequential loader/offload nodes and asks for 100 GB+ disk. [Current official requirements](https://docs.ltx.io/open-source-model/getting-started/system-requirements) specify 32 GB+ VRAM / 32 GB RAM / 100 GB storage, recommending A100 80 GB or H100; these are general requirements, not a benchmark for this exact 121-frame repair.

For a concrete paid reference, [Runpod's public pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing), checked 7 November, lists A40 **48 GB VRAM / 50 GB RAM at $0.59/hour**. Fifteen to sixty billed minutes would be **$0.1475–$0.59 compute**, before storage/tax; that is budget arithmetic, **not a predicted render duration**. Cold loading, retries and idle time count. No account availability or exact repair runtime was checked, and no paid deployment is selected or authorized. Kaggle incurs no purchase but consumes the existing free allowance; its repair quota cost remains unmeasured. None of this establishes the ten-full-videos-for-€20 target.

If this optional method is pursued for another shot, its gate is measured runtime, audited mask/source alignment, preserved unmasked pixels, and independent normal-speed anatomy/continuity review. This technical research does not override the later M01 reuse judgment.
