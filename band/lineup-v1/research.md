# Reference-pack method — 8 November 2026

This is a reusable static drawing/production pack, not proof of animation, fingering, a live sound setup, or the final performance edit.

## Demonstrated production method

[Toon Boom's character-sheet guide](https://helpcentre.toonboom.com/hc/en-ca/articles/57134612631955-Character-Design-03-Finalizing-the-character-sheets), updated 5 November 2026, separates a comparative lineup, character turnarounds, action poses and expression sheets. Turnaround landmarks should align across views. Action poses should depict movements the production actually needs. It also identifies hand/foot references and lip-sync sheets for more demanding cut-out work. We apply that separation here: establish identity and silhouette first, then instrument contact and performance positions, retaining Yaro's existing identity rather than redesigning him to match the new photographs. A model sheet is a consistency reference; it is not automatically a finished rig.

[Yamaha's stage-plot guidance](https://hub.yamaha.com/proaudio/livesound/seven-ways-to-ensure-a-smooth-sound-check/) describes a physical instrument layout with labeled performer positions and matching source identifiers. Downstage is the audience edge; drums usually occupy the rear. [Berklee's Red Room requirements](https://www.berklee.edu/red-room-cafe-939/booking-and-rental/for-bands) demonstrate that both a stage plot and input list belong in a real performance advance. For this illustrated film, the useful transfer is fixed positions and camera-readable orientation, not an invented requirement to procure a live PA or produce an audio channel list for prerecorded music.

## Review criteria applied to the generated pack

1. **Identity:** four supplied new identities remain distinct from Yaro and from each other across angle, expression and costume. The blonde guitarist is part of the new supplied lineup; the previous all-male proposal is superseded. She is not the red-haired narrative woman.
2. **Symbols:** only Dario/keyboardist wears a cross. This is an explicit user direction, not a belief inferred from a photograph. Other members retain neutral jewelry without introduced religious symbols.
3. **Turnaround:** stable height/proportions, hair shape, beard, costume closures, hem lengths and boot silhouettes; front, three-quarter, profile and rear views must describe the same body.
4. **Instrument contact:** one supported instrument per player, plausible straps/stands/stool, forearms connected to hands, fingers distinguishable at the available resolution, appropriate fret/bridge or keybed locations, sticks gripped rather than fused into wrists. Static plausibility does not establish accurate playing.
5. **Continuity:** choose an authoritative instrument design, handedness, stage costume and stage position. Pose sheets must not silently change the instrument or flip its playing side. Label variations rather than presenting them as exact matches.
6. **Stage geography:** audience-left/right unambiguous, singer visible at front, drums behind, keys reachable, enough room for guitar/bass necks and camera coverage. Do not swap screen sides between shots without a motivated camera change.
7. **Acceptance boundary:** record concrete visible defects and remedies. Successful generation and image-file validation are not user approval or motion/lip-sync clearance. New animation must still be checked against actual music and consecutive decoded frames.

Search results were followed to the primary Toon Boom, Yamaha and Berklee pages above. The Toon Boom Learn Portal model-sheet page returned HTTP403 on direct opening; the Help Centre guide was readable and supplies the method used here.

## Proposed fixed stage geography

This is an illustrated camera/blocking reference, not a measured venue or an instruction to buy equipment. Stage box: roughly 8.8 units wide × 6 deep. Use **audience coordinates** throughout: x increases from audience-left to audience-right; y increases from the audience edge toward the rear. Audience edge is y=0. Avoid switching to performer-left/right names midway through a shot list.

| Role | Position (x,y) | Facing/support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yaro, lead vocal | (0,1.2) | Downstage centre, facing audience; handheld wired mic or fixed stand specified per shot. |
| Guitar | (-2.0,1.6) | Audience-left/front, neck angled inward only when it leaves Yaro's body silhouette clear. |
| Bass | (+2.3,1.6) | Audience-right/front, neck naturally points toward audience-right for a right-handed player facing out. |
| Keys | (-3.0,3.6) | Rear audience-left, keyboard angled about 20° toward centre; player behind the keybed, audience sees front/three-quarter body. Standing or seated must be declared, not changed accidentally mid-action. |
| Drums | (0,4.6) | Rear centre on a shallow riser; seat behind snare/toms and bass drum facing audience. Raise or frame the drummer's face above Yaro's silhouette in a wide rather than shifting him between cuts. |

Keep at least a clear walking/cable lane between front line and backline. Instrument necks and cymbal edges must not intersect neighbouring faces or become extensions of another player's arms. Actual stand/riser footprints will need checking in each final stage plate; these point coordinates alone are not a collision simulation.

Camera continuity:

- Establish this geography in a wide before isolated performance inserts. The identity comparison lineup is **not** a stage plot.
- Keep normal coverage on the audience side of the band. The guitar remains on screen-left and bass on screen-right in frontal cuts. A rear camera reverses that appearance; motivate it with an establishing move or a clearly recognisable rear view.
- Suggested coverage positions are wide (0,-8), guitar (-3,-3), bass (+3,-3), keys (-3.8,0.5) and an elevated centre/side drum view. These are planning coordinates, not camera calibration data.
- For the keyboard close-up, preserve keybed orientation and the same bass/treble ends. A reverse angle must not silently mirror the instrument or swap hands.
- For guitar/bass close-ups, keep handedness stable: fretting left, picking/plucking right. Strings run from bridge to their corresponding tuners, not into the strap or sleeve.
- Use the same major drums/cymbals and seated body position across drum coverage. A close insert may crop stands, but it must not invent a new kit layout.
- Palette and lighting can change in a motivated chorus/bridge, but costume, major instrument silhouette, cross rule and musician identity remain fixed. Narrative red-haired woman remains outside the performing-band roster.
